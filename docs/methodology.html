Methodology — v1.0.0

The BugOut Index is a directional stress and stability reading. Six published metrics are placed between fixed endpoints and combined into one weighted 0–100 score. Higher means those inputs sit closer to the stable end of the ranges. The number shows how hard current published conditions are pressing. It is not a forecast, and it is not an instruction to bug out. This page describes how the current week's number was produced. Methodology version is v1.0.0.

1. Inputs and weights

Raw weights are relative coefficients that sum to 0.72. The “share of score” column is weight / 0.72 — the fraction of the finished index each metric actually carries after the publisher divides by the sum of weights. Tile labels that say “Raw weight 15%” are the raw weight × 100, not the post-division share.

2. Normalization

Each metric is normalized to 0–100 using predefined thresholds:

normalized = (1 − (raw − min) / (max − min)) × 100

Trust in Government is inverted (higher trust → higher stability) by applying 100 − normalized after the above transform — equivalently a linear map of the trust percent on 0–80. An Edelman reading of 41 normalizes to 51.25. Inflation at 4% on −10…15 normalizes to 44.

3. Aggregation

The index is the weight-normalized sum of the per-metric scores (divide by the sum of weights; with all six present that denominator is 0.72). A perfect week on every metric therefore scores 100:

BOI = Σ (normalized_i × weight_i) / Σ weight_i

4. Risk bands

These four bands and cutoffs are methodology v1.0.0. They locate where today’s score sits. They do not forecast a crisis, and they are not a siren.

5. Limitations and caveats

  • Freshness mismatch. Inflation, unemployment, and debt-to-GDP update monthly or quarterly; the Edelman trust score updates annually; homelessness is annual (HUD PIT). The BOI is recomputed weekly but will only move when underlying data publishes.
  • National scope. Crime and homelessness figures are national averages or approximations; local conditions vary substantially.
  • Not a forecast, and not a bug-out order. The index is a directional reading of published statistics relative to fixed endpoints. It shows how hard those conditions are pressing now. It does not say what happens next, and a lower band is not an instruction to leave.
  • Not a historical crisis siren. Do not judge the index mainly by whether it would have flipped in every past recession. A mechanical FRED replay of methodology v1.0.0 — inflation, unemployment, and debt-to-GDP only, with crime, homelessness, and trust excluded or held constant and labeled — is in the source repository (results at a glance). It is not a full six-metric historical BugOut Index. In that replay, a Great Recession–shaped path stays in Moderate Stability, which is consistent with “2008 should not have been bug-out level.” A COVID-scale unemployment spike moves toward Low Stability, which is closer to “take this seriously.”
  • Labor utilization is not in the score. The homepage charts prime-age employment-population ratio (FRED LNS12300060) and prime-age participation (FRED LNS11300060) as a shadow series. They are not in the BugOut Index and they have no weight. Replacing headline unemployment would be a versioned change after a backtest.
  • Food prices are not in the score. The homepage charts the 12-month percent change in food CPI (FRED CPIUFDNS, BLS CUUR0000SAF1, not seasonally adjusted) as a shadow series. It is not in the BugOut Index and it has no weight. A draft 0–10% normalization in the incubating note is not applied.
  • Threshold sensitivity. Because the index is built on fixed normalization ranges, slow drift inside a range will not move the score — the thresholds are calibrated to reflect historically extreme conditions at the endpoints.

6. Reproducibility

Every weekly run commits the raw inputs, the computed normalized scores, and the final index. Those committed files are the record of that week. The code that produced this page is open source. Rerunning runtime/publish/weekly_run.py fetches the latest observations, so it does not rebuild a past week’s score. Methodology version is v1.0.0.
