About this index

The BugOut Index is a directional stress and stability reading (methodology v1.1.1). Six published statistics sit between fixed endpoints and combine into one 0–100 score. Higher means those conditions are less stressed. The number shows how hard the published inputs are pressing. It is not a forecast, and it is not an instruction to bug out. Markets, the short-term pulse, labor utilization, food prices, the U.S. regional shelter companions, Pew public trust, and Gallup confidence in institutions on this page are companions. They are not in the score. Edelman remains the trust input. The shelter readings are local: New York City, Nashville–Davidson, Austin/Travis County, Ramsey County (Minnesota), Denver, and San Francisco. None of them is a U.S. total. Denver and San Francisco are occupancy rates, not headcounts. HUD AHAR remains the annual national homelessness input. Several inputs update monthly, quarterly, or once a year, so many weeks the number does not move. Crime under methodology 1.1.0 is the Real-Time Crime Index Nationwide Full Sample rate for the latest month in the file. Weeks through 2026-10-02 stay methodology 1.0.0, with crime locked at 2,723.0. See methodology for the calculation, weights, and limits. The committed weekly files are the record of a past score. Rerunning the publisher fetches current observations and does not rebuild that week.

For the long-horizon structural picture — whether the country is currently set up to produce broadly-shared prosperity and mobility over years and decades — see the American Dream Reality Index (ADRI). ADRI updates annually and weights toward education, health, real income, employment, inequality, and civic and safety fundamentals. The two indexes answer different questions. The BugOut Index is a weekly reading of pressure in a few published series. ADRI moves on slow structural conditions. Each can move for reasons the other does not catch.
